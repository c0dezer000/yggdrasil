# Council Protocol — "the Thing"

Structured adversarial deliberation. Expensive (5–8× a normal loop); reserved for the classes below.

## When to convene

Architecture / ADR-class decisions · money, legal, or compliance questions · irreversible changes · contested root-cause analysis · the Execution Gate · any high-stakes-register question. **Never for routine tasks.**

## Procedure

1. **Frame.** State the question, constraints, and what a decision must produce. A vague question yields fake consensus.
2. **Seat.** 3–5 roles by relevance, **plus one seat assigned the opposition brief** — its job is the strongest case *against* the proposal, regardless of its private view.
3. **Round 1 — independent positions.** Each seat is invoked **without seeing any other seat's answer.** This is the most important rule: the moment one seat reads another, anchoring begins and the exercise becomes theatre.
4. **Steelman.** Each seat restates the strongest opposing position before rebutting it. Prevents strawman theatre; often reveals the disagreement was definitional.
5. **Cross-examination.** At most 2 rounds. Positions may change; changes state what evidence moved them.
6. **Decision memo** (written by the controller role): question · seat composition (roles **and models**) · positions in brief · points of agreement · **live disagreement** with each side's concessions · recommendation and reasoning · **dissent preserved verbatim** with the condition under which the dissenter would be proven right · **gardener decision required.** The memo never decides.

## Integrity rules

- **Unanimous round 1 is flagged**, not celebrated: *"unanimous first-round agreement — likely anchoring or an under-specified question."*
- **Model diversity where possible.** Different models produce genuine disagreement; one model produces one opinion in several voices.
- **Single-model councils are labeled:** *"single-model council — structured self-check, not independent review."*
- The opposition seat may run on a cheap or local model — hole-finding needs skepticism more than brilliance.
- Dissent is never resolved into consensus for tidiness. Preserved dissent is the memo's most valuable content six months later.
